# Subject motion and creative review

Use for animated objects/characters, cinematic channel samples, or a user who rejects
slide-like imagery, monotonous pacing, crude drawing or poor visual quality.

## Diagnose the visual problem before another export

Distinguish asset craft, subject movement, shot design and edit rhythm. More keyframes
do not repair weak drawing, floating props, detached hands, mechanical bodies or an
unmotivated sequence. If the user rejects the visual form, choose a materially different
production approach; do not keep polishing the same primitive stand-ins and call the
result premium.

Match the renderer to the intended look. Diagrams and original designed graphics can
use code; people, tactile food close-ups and documentary-style motion may benefit from
designed/raster assets, real permitted footage or a suitable video generator. Verify
the actual tool, account and existing credits before a provider attempt. Do not buy
credits, install libraries or substitute providers merely because this reference exists.

## A shot needs an observable event

Write the action and its outcome: a fork pushes a portion into a spoon; the loaded
spoon lifts; palms meet before a bow; a person reaches for a glass. Specify contact,
support, entry/exit and what carries a prop. Use the animation-principles skill for
rigs and the actual production workflow for generated footage.

Use preparation, acceleration, contact, settling and purposeful rests where the action
calls for them. Keep the camera subordinate to the event. Change framing when it
reveals new information; do not use periodic cuts, ambient wobble, arbitrary zooms or
large title panels as substitutes for subject movement. Captions support the visual
story rather than occupying the focal area.

## Review a representative shot before batching

Render a short moving proof that includes the hardest contact/gesture and a camera
change, at the intended aspect and usable resolution. Inspect continuous playback
when the available tools support it, plus dense frames through contact and transition
intervals. If only frames can be inspected, report that limitation; sampled stills
alone cannot establish convincing motion or absence of penetrations throughout.

Check silhouette, hand/prop contact, object permanence, weight, pacing, visual hierarchy,
caption distraction and whether the action is understandable with narration muted.
Revise failures before spending on or rendering the rest of a batch. The agent can do
this review autonomously; ask for user approval only if the user requested that gate
or a real permission/cost decision is needed.

## Keep evidence honest

Separate technical acceptance (streams, decode, deterministic seeking, levels, ASR)
from visual/editorial assessment and human listening. A low motionless-frame count
or high pixel difference can be caused by captions, camera noise or decorative motion;
neither proves quality. Retain rejected versions as history, mark them superseded,
and deliver the actual improved moving proof rather than another plan or contact sheet.
