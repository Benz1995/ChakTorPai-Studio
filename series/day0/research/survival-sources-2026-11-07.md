# Survival-lesson sources for "วันที่ 0" (PolyTale) — research of 2026-11-07

Method: every page below was opened and read in full by the research agent (WebFetch or the built-in browser; the two Thai PDFs were downloaded to a scratch folder and their text layers extracted). Quotes are close paraphrase unless in quotation marks. "NOT READ" marks things seen only as search snippets and therefore not to be cited in the show.

Reading limits (be honest on screen and in credits):
- IAEA: no lay-public "what to do" page could be opened. iaea.org "emergency preparedness" topic URL 404s; IAEA publication PDFs return HTTP 402 or force a download. Only one IAEA page was read in full (responder guideline, radiological/dirty-bomb scenario, not a nuclear detonation). IAEA claims from search snippets are listed separately as NOT READ.
- Thai DDPM (ปภ.): the dedicated site pages (ddpm.go.th does not resolve; disaster.go.th home page only lists topic names). One real DDPM leaflet (flood, ถุงยังชีพ) was read. The "72-hour emergency bag / 6 categories" list was read only in a Thai PBS news article that cites DDPM without a link.
- Thai Cell Broadcast: only news/search summaries; the PRD (กรมประชาสัมพันธ์) page URLs returned 404. Not read at source.
- Thai MOPH / กรมควบคุมโรค: search found no public radiation-emergency guidance.

## 1. WARNING RECEIVED: where to shelter, how fast

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| Ready.gov "Radiation Emergencies" (https://www.ready.gov/radiation, last updated 01/07/2025) | "If warned of the possibility of a radiation hazard, immediately get inside the nearest building and move away from windows. Put as many walls between you and the outside." Nuclear detonation: "you will have 10 minutes or more to find an adequate shelter before fallout arrives." If a multi-story building or basement "can be safely reached within a few minutes of the explosion, go there immediately. The safest buildings have brick or concrete walls. Underground parking garages and subways can also provide good shelter." Most protective location: "basement or center of a large building." Principle: time, distance, shielding. |
| CDC "What to Do: Get Inside" (https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/response/get-inside.html, 15 Apr 2024) | Get inside as soon as possible. Go to "the basement or the middle of the building, away from the walls and roof, since radiation settles on the outside of buildings." If outside or in a vehicle: get into a building right away; brick or concrete multi-story or basement is best, "any building is safer than being outside." Gives NO number of minutes. Pets inside too. |
| CDC "Preparing for a Radiation Emergency" (https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/safety/index.html, 31 Jan 2025) | Same: get inside, stay inside, stay tuned; basement or middle of building, away from walls and roof. |
| WHO Q&A "Radiation and health" (https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/radiation-and-health, 7 Jul 2023) | "Get in, Stay in and Tune in." Walls and ceilings shield from outdoor fallout. If possible use a room with no windows or external doors. |
| IAEA first-responder "Public protection guidelines" (https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/publications/pdf/epr-first_res-pda/html/ti3.htm; responder tool, undated page) | Radiological incident (smoke/dust), not a nuclear weapon. ## 2. OUTDOORS AT THE FLASH / BLAST

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| Ready.gov (/radiation) | "If you have warning, take cover from the blast behind anything that might offer protection. If you are outside, lie face down to protect exposed skin from the heat and flying debris. After the shockwave passes, go inside the nearest building as quickly as possible." |
| CDC "Nuclear Blasts: FAQ" (https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/about/nuclear-blast-faq.html, 10 Apr 2024) | Near the blast: "Turn away, cover your eyes, and lie face down with your hands under your body until the heat and two shock waves pass." Outdoors: cover nose and mouth, brush dust off clothing in a ventilated area, move to an underground space, upwind if possible; remove contaminated clothing and wash if you can. Mentions burns and eye damage possible depending on distance. |
| CDC "Get Inside" | If you cannot get inside immediately, cover mouth and nose with a mask, cloth or towel. |
| WHO, IAEA, OAP, DDPM | Say nothing about the flash/blast moment. |

NOTE: "two shock waves" and "lie face down hands under body" are CDC only; Ready.gov says only "lie face down". "Don't look at the flash" is not stated as such; CDC says "turn away, cover your eyes".

## 5. SELF-DECONTAMINATION

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| CDC "How to Self-Decontaminate" (https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/prevention/self-decontaminate.html, 15 Apr 2024) | 1) "Taking off your outer layer of clothing can remove up to 90% of radioactive material"; undress carefully, do not breathe shaken-off dust; seal clothes in plastic bag or container away from people/pets. 2) No shower: wash hands, face, uncovered skin with soap and plenty of water at a sink, or use a moist wipe/clean wet cloth/damp paper towel; gently blow nose, wipe eyelids, eyelashes, ears; bag the used cloths. 3) Shower if you can: warm shower, plenty of soap, no scalding/scrubbing/scratching; "Wash your hair with shampoo or soap." 4) Keep cuts and abrasions covered while washing. Conditioner is NOT mentioned. Tap water is fine for washing even if not for drinking. |
| Ready.gov (/radiation) | Same 90% line; careful removal; avoid touching eyes/nose/mouth; shower or wash with soap and water; if you cannot, wipe skin and hair with a wipe or clean wet cloth; "Do not use household cleaning wipes on your skin. Hand sanitizer does not protect against radioactive material." Brush and wash pets. |
| CDC "Get Inside" | Before entering remove outer clothing if you can; wash uncovered body parts; put on clean clothes. |
| OAP booklet p.9 "แนวทางการชำระล้างการเปื้อนทางรังสี" (Thai-specific, the only source with minute counts) | Skin/hands/body: soap and water, wash suspected area 2-3 minutes, 2 times; or soap + soft brush + water, 2 minutes, 3 times; or dry soap/detergent powder mixed with water for hands, scrub gently. Eyes: flip eyelid, rinse with gently flowing water. Ears: clean ear canal with cotton. Mouth: rinse and gargle, "ห้ามกลืนน้ำเด็ดขาด". Hair: soap and water, wash 2 minutes, 3 times; or soap + soft brush; "กรรไกรตัดผมหรือเครื่องตัดผม" cut hair off if scalp contamination persists, then treat like skin. Conditioner not mentioned. Wounds: not mentioned. Does not mention removing clothes in that table. |
| IAEA responder page | "wash hands, shower and change clothes when possible", place clothes in a plastic bag and keep; do not smoke, eat, drink or touch mouth until showered/changed. |
| WHO | Not covered (mentions skin/hair/clothing contamination only as an exposure type). |

Conditioner: NO official page read (CDC, Ready.gov, WHO, IAEA, OAP) mentions hair conditioner. CDC says shampoo or soap; OAP says soap. The "do not use conditioner" rule is widespread online (it is repeated in US emergency-planning material by individual experts) but is not on any page read here.

## 6. FOOD / WATER SAFETY; WATER QUANTITY FOR A KIT; KI

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| Ready.gov (/radiation) | Food/drink that was inside a building is safe. Sealed containers that were outside are safe if wiped with a damp cloth first (bag the cloth). Unsealed food from outside may be contaminated. "Do not consume food from your garden, or food or liquids that were outdoors and uncovered, until authorities tell you it is safe." KI is NOT mentioned on this page. |
| CDC "Stay Inside" | Sealed foods/drinks or those already inside are safe. Officials will test drinking water and confirm when safe. "Boiling tap water will not remove radioactive materials." Washing with tap water is safe. Medicines indoors: keep taking. |
| CDC "Nuclear Blasts FAQ" | Use stored food and water; avoid local fresh food and open water sources. |
| Ready.gov "Build A Kit" (https://www.ready.gov/kit, updated 07/01/2026) | Water: "one gallon per person per day for several days, for drinking and sanitation" (about 3.8 L). Flashlight, first aid kit, batteries, whistle, moist towelettes, garbage bags and ties, manual can opener, phone chargers; additions include medicines, change of clothes, sturdy shoes, cash, documents. (The page lists no KI.) The Ready.gov radiation page adds for radiation: spare change of clothes per person, a trash bag for contaminated clothing, sealed food, water, medicine. |
| CDC KI page (https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/treatment/potassium-iodide.html, 30 Jan 2025) | "Do not take KI unless instructed by public health or emergency response officials or a healthcare provider." Recommended for people under 40 and pregnant/breastfeeding women; over 40 only if officials say predicted exposure is high. Take within 24 hours before or 4 hours after exposure for best effect; one dose protects 24 hours. Protects only the thyroid, only against radioactive iodine; "does not protect against the external radiation from fallout after a nuclear detonation." Not a substitute: table salt or iodine foods; supplements may not work. Taking more than recommended can cause severe illness. CDC Nuclear Blast FAQ: local officials decide whether and when. |
| WHO "Use of potassium iodide for thyroid protection ..." (https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/use-of-potassium-iodide-for-thyroid-protection-during-nuclear-or-radiological-emergencies, 8 Jul 2025) | "KI should not be taken as a generic protective measure in anticipation of an event." Take only when authorities instruct. Children are the priority group; adults over 40 generally not indicated (except emergency personnel). Optimal timing: less than 24 h before up to 2 h after exposure start; up to 8 h after still reasonable; after 24 h no protection. One dose protects about 24 h. Pregnant/breastfeeding women: usually a single dose. Should be combined with sheltering and stopping consumption of contaminated food/water. Authorities announce via radio, TV, internet, loudspeakers. |
| WHO "Radiation and health" | KI protects only the thyroid; reduces thyroid cancer risk in people aged 0-18 exposed to radioactive iodine; only when local authorities instruct. |
| OAP booklet | Food: follow government bans on consuming goods/products from affected areas (initial phase abroad: dust; medium/long term 1 month-1 year: food contamination). NO mention of KI, no water quantity. |
| DDPM leaflet "การเตรียมพร้อมรับมืออุทกภัย / การจัดเตรียมถุงยังชีพ" (https://backofficeminisite.disaster.go.th/apiv1/apps/minisite_ckg/203/content/4848/download?filename=259906fe75ccb82413807004cf730c03.pdf, undated, flood-specific) | ถุงยังชีพ for "อย่างน้อย 3 - 5 วัน". # ANALYSIS

## Overall
The US pages (Ready.gov, CDC) are written for a nuclear detonation or any radiation emergency and are the only sources with concrete time/place detail. WHO gives short, consistent confirmation and the best KI detail. The Thai authority OAP's public booklet is shorter and generic, written mostly for Thai incidents (lost/abandoned radioactive sources, fires) and for fallout drifting from abroad; it is the only source with minute-by-minute washing instructions. Nothing official found is written for Thai viewers about a nuclear weapon strike, a Thai shelter network, or a Thai alert for it.

## Topic 1: Warning received
Agree: get inside the nearest building now, stay away from windows/outer walls, more walls and mass between you and outside is better (Ready.gov, CDC, WHO, IAEA-responder). 
Differ: only Ready.gov/CDC give a building ranking (brick/concrete, basement, center of large building, parking garage/subway); WHO prefers a windowless room without external doors; OAP gives none. Only Ready.gov gives "10 minutes or more" (US detonation planning figure); CDC gives no minutes. The "10 minutes" is for fallout arrival after a ground burst, not for blast arrival, and depends on distance and wind.
Tell Thai viewers: "ถ้าได้รับเตือน เข้าอาคารที่ใกล้ที่สุดทันที ยิ่งอยู่ลึกยิ่งดี ห่างหน้าต่างและผนังด้านนอก ถ้ามีชั้นใต้ดิน/ห้องกลางอาคารให้ไปที่นั่น". Say "นาที ไม่ใช่ชั่วโมง" rather than quoting 10 minutes as a promise; if the number is shown, credit it to Ready.gov (US). Thai authority: OAP says follow government announcements (hotline 1296, 24 h); DDPM 1784.
Not supported: any claim that a particular Thai building type (e.g., Thai townhouse) is safe; no Thai source assesses this.

## Topic 2: Outdoors at the flash/blast
Agree (Ready.gov + CDC only): take cover from the blast, lie face down, protect exposed skin; after shockwave passes go inside quickly. 
Differ: CDC adds turn away/cover eyes, hands under body, "two shock waves", cover nose and mouth, move upwind and underground. Ready.gov is shorter. Neither WHO, IAEA, OAP nor DDPM covers it.
Tell Thai viewers: this is US-source advice; credit CDC/Ready.gov. Do not credit Thai authorities. 
Unsupported/over-stated online: "get under a desk" or "count seconds to the shockwave" as official numbers (not on any page read).

## Topic 3: Duration, decision-maker, family
Agree: authorities decide when to leave; do not leave early. Ready.gov, CDC: at least 24 hours (US figure for nuclear detonation); Ready.gov lists exceptions (fire, gas leak, collapse, serious injury). OAP: stay until the government confirms (no hour count). 
Differ: 24 h is a detonation/fallout-decay figure; OAP's phases for abroad-event dust are "48 hours - 7 days" for dust arrival and 1 month - 1 year for food. A reactor accident can need different durations (IAEA snippet "sheltering a day or less", NOT READ).
Family: only CDC says it explicitly (going out to get loved ones could expose you and them; schools/hospitals have plans). Ready.gov is silent on family retrieval.
Tell Thai viewers: "อยู่ในที่หลบอย่างน้อย 24 ชั่วโมง และรอประกาศทางการก่อนออก" with a clear note that 24 h is the CDC/Ready.gov number. For family: "อย่าออกไปตามหาคนในครอบครัว โรงเรียน/โรงพยาบาลมีแผนของตัวเอง" credited to CDC. Thai authority/channel: OAP (1296) for radiation announcements; DDPM (1784) and Thai emergency alerts. 
Unsupported online: "exactly 48 hours", "3 days", or "14 days" shelter rules; none read on official pages (the "48 h–7 d" OAP figure is the arrival window of dust, not a shelter instruction).

## Topic 4: Shelter actions
Agree: close windows and doors; turn off fans/AC/heating that pull outside air; keep radio on for official information (Ready.gov, CDC, WHO). 
Differ: CDC says "close and lock"; CDC FAQ adds seal and "then open some windows for air" after fallout passes; Ready.gov and WHO don't. Ready.gov Kit lists plastic sheeting and duct tape "to shelter in place" but the shelter-in-place instructions do not say to seal gaps with tape. NOAA weather radio is US-specific; the generic point is battery/hand-crank radio. CDC warns an EMP may damage electronics. 
Tell Thai viewers: close windows/doors, switch off AC and fans, battery or hand-crank radio, use SMS instead of calls, follow OAP/ปภ./government channels. Do not show an air-conditioner left running as "safe".
Thai channel: no Thai document names which radio/TV station or app for radiological alerts. Cell Broadcast exists (news-reported) but no official source tying it to radiation. Say "ติดตามประกาศทางการ" instead of naming a station.
Unsupported online: wrapping every gap in duct tape and plastic as a standard nuclear-shelter instruction (it is in Ready.gov's kit list only as a general sheltering item).

## Topic 5: Self-decontamination
Agree: remove outer clothing (up to 90% of radioactive material, CDC and Ready.gov), carefully and without shaking; bag it away from people/pets; wash skin and hair with soap and water; wipe if no shower; do not scrub hard. 
Differ: CDC (shower, shampoo or soap, wipe eyes/ears, cover wounds) vs OAP (minutes: skin 2-3 min x2, or 2 min x3 with soft brush; hair 2 min x3; cut hair if scalp stays contaminated; eyes/ears/mouth rinse). Ready.gov alone warns hand sanitizer and household wipes don't work. IAEA responder: also don't eat/drink/smoke until washed.
Tell Thai viewers: remove outer clothes first, bag them, wash with soap and water (OAP numbers can be shown credited to ปส.), keep cuts covered (CDC), do not scrub or scald, no hand sanitizer. 
Flag: "use only shampoo, never conditioner" is not in CDC, Ready.gov, WHO, IAEA or OAP pages read. If the show says it, credit it as "คำแนะนำที่พบบ่อย ไม่ใช่ข้อความจากหน่วยงาน" or omit. Also: "cut hair" is OAP only, as a last resort for stubborn scalp contamination; "wash wounds" isn't official either (CDC says keep cuts covered). Iodine skin painting, bleach, or "Geiger-counter-free" tricks are unsupported.

## Topic 6: Food/water and KI
Agree: indoor/sealed food and drink are safe; do not eat unsealed outdoor food or garden produce until authorities say it is safe (Ready.gov, CDC, IAEA responder incl. vegetables/rainwater, OAP bans per government). Tap-water decisions come from authorities; CDC: boiling doesn't remove radioactive material. KI: only when authorities instruct; protects thyroid only; not a general protection (CDC, WHO).
Differ on water quantity: Ready.gov 1 gallon (~3.8 L)/person/day for several days (drinking AND sanitation); Thai PBS (citing ปภ./Sphere/FEMA) 3 L/person/day for 72 h; DDPM leaflet says 3-5 days and gives no volume. KI timing: CDC "24 h before to 4 h after"; WHO "<24 h before to 2 h after, up to 8 h still reasonable"; age cutoffs: CDC under 40 (over 40 only if high exposure); WHO children priority, adults over 40 generally not. US detonation: CDC says KI does not protect against fallout external radiation; KI matters mostly in reactor/radioiodine releases. 
Tell Thai viewers: store water for at least 3 days (3 L per person per day is the Thai-media DDPM number; Ready.gov's 1 gallon is bigger and includes sanitation); eat only sealed or indoor food; wait for official water/food announcements. KI: do not buy and take it on your own; Thai sources (OAP booklet, DDPM) do not mention KI, so say: "ยาเม็ดไอโอดีนกินเมื่อทางการสั่งเท่านั้น (ตามองค์การอนามัยโลก/CDC) ใช้ปกป้องต่อมไทรอยด์เท่านั้น ไม่ใช่ยากันรังสี และเกลือเสริมไอโอดีนใช้แทนไม่ได้". WHO: iodized salt is not a substitute.
Unsupported online: "eat/drink iodized salt or seaweed to protect yourself" (CDC and WHO explicitly reject it); "boil water to make it safe" (CDC says it doesn't remove radioactive material); "canned food is always safe" (Ready.gov: safe if sealed and wiped, so an unwiped can from outdoors is not guaranteed).

## Where US detonation guidance and reactor-accident guidance differ (matters for a Thai story)
- Detonation: blast first, fallout within minutes-to-hours, shelter at least 24 h, KI mostly irrelevant. 
- Reactor accident (IAEA/WHO/CDC nuclear-plant line): no weapon blast; shelter, then KI if told, evacuation decided by authorities, longer-term food controls. Thailand has no power reactor; the OAP booklet frames events abroad as dust drifting in (48 h - 7 days), so a Thai viewer's more likely experience is "stay inside, follow ปส., avoid certain foods", not a 24 h basement stay. The show's strike scenario should present the 24 h/basement material as US-source advice for a nearby detonation.

## What a Thai viewer should be told: the channel
Primary Thai authorities documented in this research: สำนักงานปรมาณูเพื่อสันติ (ปส./OAP), hotline 1296 (24 h), 0 2596 7600, www.oap.go.th; กรมป้องกันและบรรเทาสาธารณภัย (ปภ.), สายด่วนนิรภัย 1784, www.disaster.go.th. Thai Cell Broadcast is news-reported only. State plainly in the show that Thailand has no published public "nuclear attack" shelter instruction found; the lessons are drawn from CDC, Ready.gov, WHO, and OAP/ปภ. where they say something.

## Claims commonly repeated online that no official page read supports
1. Conditioner binds radiation to hair, so use none (no official page mentions conditioner).
2. Iodized salt or iodine-rich food protects against radiation (rejected by CDC, WHO).
3. Boiling water makes it safe (CDC: no).
4. Seal windows with duct tape and plastic as the standard rule (only in the kit list).
5. Exact "shelter 48 hours / 3 days / 14 days" (only 24 h appears, CDC/Ready.gov; OAP uses "until the government confirms").
6. "Fallout arrives in 15 minutes / you have 30 minutes" (only "10 minutes or more", Ready.gov).
7. "Hide in a bathtub / under a car" as official guidance (not on any page read).
8. "Shower with hand sanitizer / bleach" (Ready.gov: hand sanitizer doesn't protect).
9. A Thai government text telling people to take KI tablets (none found).
